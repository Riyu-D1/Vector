# SOMA carrier board: pin-by-pin and placement verification before ordering

Context: the routed 135 x 115 mm board (commit 01a4a60) passes KiCad DRC electrically. The user now wants proof that every pin does what it should, nothing overlaps or collides in real life, and the board can be fabricated and soldered with no surprises. Treat this as an audit by someone who distrusts the generator. Do not assume the datasheet comments inside gen_pcb.py are right: check against the real documents (Espressif docs for the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 v1.x pinout, the servo and module datasheets or product pages already used in docs/BOM.md, and the KiCad footprint pad numbering). Cite the source (URL or file) for each claim. If you cannot find a source for a pinout, say "unverified" in the table, never guess.

Hard rules: do not change anything until the full audit is written. Fix only real findings. If a fix moves a part, re-run the whole pipeline once (check_pads.py, route, fill_zones.py, DRC, gerbers) and re-verify the affected rows. No new router experiments. Keep the 60 s cap per leg.

## Phase 1: pin-by-pin netlist audit  -> docs/pcb-verification.md, section "Pins"
For EVERY component and EVERY pad in the final .kicad_pcb write a row: ref, pad number, net, what the real part needs on that pin, source, PASS or FAIL. Generate it with a script (`pcb/audit_pins.py`) from the emitted file, then check the "what it needs" column by reading datasheets, not by trusting the generator. Cover at least:
1. ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 sockets U1A and U1B: all 44 pins. Pin 1 must be at the USB (west) end; confirm J1 and J3 column order against Espressif's pinout diagram. Confirm 5V, 3V3, GND, RST are on the right physical pins, that no GPIO used for a signal is a strapping pin (0, 3, 45, 46), USB (19, 20), console (43, 44) or flash and PSRAM (26 to 37 on the N16R8 module), and VSNS is an ADC1 pin. Confirm the two sockets are 22.86 mm apart (module header row spacing) and the module is not mirrored.
2. Each servo bus port J4, J5, J6 (KK-254 3-pin): GND, +12V, DATA order against the STS3215 and HTD-45H cable pinouts in docs/BOM.md, and that BUSA and BUSB data really reach the pins the firmware plan uses.
3. XT60 J16 polarity (+ and - the right way round for an XT60 pair, check against the footprint pad 1 and 2 marking), screw terminals J1 and J2 (which is 12 V IN, which is 5 V OUT, + and - not swapped), power-enable jumper JP1, fuse F1, J3 (Mini-360 optional module header, pin order against the Mini-360 module: IN+, IN-, OUT+, OUT-).
4. Every sensor and module header: OLED J11 (SSD1306 I2C module order is usually GND, VCC, SCL, SDA but some are VCC, GND: state which one the BOM part is), IMU J12 (GY-521 pin order), KY-040 J13 (CLK, DT, SW, +, GND), estop J9, WRIST UART J10 (TX/RX crossover: ESP32 TX must reach the wrist node RX), I2C ports J7 and J8, spare J15, NeoPixel J14, IR receiver IR1 (VS1838B order OUT, GND, VCC), temperature sensor (DS18B20 TO-92 order and its 4.7 k pull-up present), buzzer BZ1 and its driver transistor Q1 (2N2222: say whether the footprint is EBC or CBE and whether the part in the BOM matches; some are swapped between makers).
5. Polarised parts: D1 (5V feed Schottky: anode to +5V, cathode to +5V_MCU, current flows into the ESP32), D4 (TVS 15 V on 12 V IN: it must be a unidirectional TVS with cathode to +12V and anode to GND; check the band side on the silkscreen matches), LEDs D2 and D3 (anode and cathode against the footprint and the resistor), electrolytic caps C1 and C3 (+ pad to the positive rail, check the silkscreen +), buzzer + pin. For each, say which physical pad is the + / anode and how it is marked on silkscreen.
6. Resistors: list the value and purpose for R1 to R8 (pull-ups, LED limit, VSNS divider) and recompute: the VSNS divider must keep the ADC pin below 3.3 V at the maximum expected 12 V rail (with the 15 V TVS clamp, so the worst case is 15 V), LED currents about 5 to 10 mA from 3.3 V or 5 V as wired.
7. Net isolation: print, for every net, the number of pads and confirm the copper really joins them all (DRC unconnected 0) and that no two nets touch (shorting 0). Also state that +12V, +5V and +3V3 are not connected to each other except through the intended parts (D1, JP1, F1).

## Phase 2: physical fit and overlap audit -> section "Placement"
No overlap of any kind is acceptable. Build a table with a computed number, not an impression.
1. Courtyards: run DRC with courtyard checks on; there must be 0 courtyards_overlap and 0 pth_inside_courtyard. Also compute, with a script, the minimum distance between the courtyard rectangles of every pair of parts and list the 10 tightest pairs.
2. Modules that sit ON the board (the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 is 69.6 x 25.4 mm and sits on female headers, about 8.5 mm above the board; the OLED, the IMU, the Mini-360, the KY-040 and the DS18B20 are on headers too). For each module define its footprint rectangle and height above the board, then list every other part that falls underneath it or sticks up into it, with part height and the free height. In particular: the DevKitC module overhangs the socket rows by roughly 8 mm at each end. Check F1 (fuse holder at about 17,50), JP1, J16, J1, J2, TP1, TP2, R7, R8, R6, R1, R2, R3, R4, D1 and D4 against the module rectangle, and say which of them are under it, how tall each one is and whether it leaves at least 2 mm to the module underside. The USB connectors at the west end of the module must have clear space around them for a cable (at least 15 mm free in the cable direction, nothing taller than 3 mm in the way, and not blocked by the case wall: wall is 2.6 mm, board edge is at x=0).
3. Tall parts against the case: C1 and C3 (electrolytics), terminal blocks, XT60, buzzer BZ1, the headers with modules on top. The tray rim is 36 mm above the floor, the board sits on 8 mm standoffs, lid thickness 4 mm. Compute, for each tall part, its top height and the clearance to the lid underside. Anything within 2 mm of the lid is a finding.
4. Edge and mounting: all copper at least 0.5 mm from the board edge; mounting holes 3.2 mm with 4 mm in from corners; standoff positions in case.py equal hole positions; M3 screw head clearance (6 mm circle free of copper that is not GND on the top side).
5. Case cutouts (cad/lib/case.py): each connector that must be reachable (XT60, screw terminals, J13 jog, J9 estop, J10 wrist, J11 OLED, USB end of the ESP32) lines up with a cutout or the lid opening. Print each connector's board position, its mapped case position and the cutout rectangle. Flag any connector that is behind a wall.
6. Silkscreen: 61 silk overlaps and 12 silk-over-copper warnings are in DRC. Fix them: no text over a pad, over a hole, or over another text, all reference text at least 0.8 mm high, polarity marks and pin 1 marks visible and not under a part body.
7. Assembly order: confirm every part can physically be soldered and inserted (no through-hole part body blocks a neighbour's pad, no header pin hidden under a diode, hand-solder access to every pad).

## Phase 3: electrical and fabrication audit -> sections "Current" and "Fab"
1. Current capacity (IPC-2221, 1 oz outer copper, 10 C rise): list each power net's minimum track width and worst current. The fuse is 6 A. The servo bus can draw several amps. State for +12V_IN, +12V_SW, +12V and the GND return the narrowest section, what current it can carry, and the weak point. If the +12V trunk is narrower than about 2.5 mm anywhere on the 6 A path, fix it by widening or by putting a pour on it, or write clearly what total servo current the board is safe for. Do not state a safe current without showing the calculation.
2. Holes: for each through-hole part, lead diameter versus drill size (the drill must be lead + 0.2 to 0.3 mm), pad annular ring at least 0.25 mm, via 0.3 mm drill with 0.7 mm pad (JLCPCB's minimum is 0.3 mm drill and 0.6 mm pad, so this is fine, confirm), minimum trace and spacing 0.2 mm (fine for JLCPCB 2-layer).
3. GND pour: list every pad on GND and say whether it has thermal relief spokes (hand soldering) and whether any GND pad is only connected through one 0.25 mm spoke or is in an island (DRC shows 29 isolated copper islands and 1 unconnected corner sliver). Remove the islands and the corner sliver or explain each in one line.
4. Gerbers: regenerate from the final board with `pcb/fab.sh`, then check with kicad-cli that the file list includes F.Cu, B.Cu, F.Mask, B.Mask, F.Silk, B.Silk, Edge.Cuts and the drill, that the zone fills appear in the copper layers, and that the board outline is 135 x 115. Zip them as `pcb/soma-carrier-gerbers.zip`. Re-render top.png and bottom.png and 3D renders from the same final file.
5. Bill of materials: from the board, make `docs/bom-from-board.csv` (ref, value, footprint, quantity). Every footprint must match a real part in docs/BOM.md (hole spacing and body size of that exact part: diode DO-201 12.7 mm, 0207 resistors, the KK-254 connector, the 10 mm and 8 mm electrolytics). Flag any part whose real dimensions differ from the footprint.

## Output (all required)
1. `docs/pcb-verification.md` with the tables above, a PASS / FAIL count per phase, and a numbered list of every finding with severity (blocker, should fix, cosmetic) and what you changed.
2. Fix every blocker and every "should fix". Re-run what is needed. Commit and push with a message that lists the fixes.
3. A final summary to the user in 10 lines or fewer: ready to order Y or N, the blockers if N, the 3 riskiest remaining items, and what the user must check by hand on the first power-up (voltage on the +5V and +3V3 rails with nothing plugged in, current limit on the bench supply, JP1 left open until then). This is a 12 V board with up to 6 A: no mains is involved, but a short can burn tracks and parts, so the first power-up is through a current-limited supply or at least a fuse.
